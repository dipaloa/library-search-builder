Build a Better Library Search
Search the Library

Turn your research question into a focused search using keywords, synonyms, and Boolean operators.

1

Start with Your Research Question

Start by putting your research question into your own words. Ask yourself: what do I want to know about this topic? Don't worry about making it sound like a database search yet. Type your research question and select Identify Key Concepts.

Example:
How does social media use affect college students' sleep?
2

Identify Your Key Concepts and Their Synonyms

Break your question into the major ideas you need to find information about. Usually 2–4 concepts are enough. Databases don't know that two different words can describe the same idea. Add alternate terms to make your search more comprehensive.

Enter your research question above and click Identify Key Concepts.

Example:
College students + social media + sleep
Examples of Synonyms:

Social media
social networking · Instagram · Facebook · Snapchat

College students
undergraduates · university students · postsecondary students

Sleep
sleeping · sleep quality · rest · insomnia
3

Build Your Search

Your terms are automatically combined into a Boolean search. You can edit the search yourself before running it.

OR Use OR between synonyms and related terms.
AND Use AND to connect different concepts.
"Quotes" Use quotation marks around exact phrases.

You can edit this search manually. Use quotation marks around phrases and an asterisk (*) for truncation.
